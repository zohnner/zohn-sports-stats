## Path A — General sports ad network (start here)

No gambling licensing exposure, no state-by-state compliance question, real self-serve or sales-assisted application. Revenue is smaller per-visitor than a sportsbook deal but the path to "live" is measured in days, not months.

**Playwire** — ad monetization network that specifically markets itself to sports/gaming/entertainment publishers (the closest niche fit of any network researched). No published minimum traffic threshold found; historically caters to higher-traffic sites, so apply directly and ask rather than assume a cutoff. [Playwire — Best Ad Monetization Platform for Sports Publishers](https://www.playwire.com/blog/best-ad-monetization-platform-for-sports-publishers)

**Raptive (formerly AdThrive)** — lowered its entry threshold in 2026 from 100k to **25k monthly pageviews**. Broad-niche network, not sports-specific, but a real, well-known payout tier. [AdThrive Requirements Guide 2026](https://monetizehelper.com/blog/adthrive-requirements-guide)

**Mediavine** — moved away from a flat session-count minimum in 2026; now evaluates by revenue, requiring roughly **$5,000+ in existing annual ad revenue** to apply directly, with a separate on-ramp program for sites below that bar. Skews lifestyle/food/finance niches over sports, so likely a lower RPM fit than Playwire even if you qualify. [Mediavine Requirements 2026](https://bloggingexplorer.com/mediavine-requirements/)

**Blocker:** every one of these gates on real traffic or existing ad revenue. **I need current site numbers (Search Console, Analytics, whatever you have) before any of these applications are worth submitting.**

## Path B — Sportsbook affiliate programs (real revenue, real legal complexity)

This is a CPA/revenue-share **affiliate** relationship, not a flat-fee "sponsorship" — that's the actual product these companies sell to sites our size. A few real, important constraints found in research, not assumptions:

- **DraftKings and FanDuel both run real affiliate programs** open to sports content sites, with dashboards, tracking links, and marketing materials. Typical commission structure researched: 25–40% of lifetime referred-player revenue (DraftKings), no negative carryover. [DraftKings Affiliate Program 2026](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/draftkings/) · [FanDuel Affiliate Program 2026](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/fanduel/)
- **BetMGM** runs its program through BetMGMPartners.com; commission plans require a signed agreement before going live. [BetMGM Affiliate Program](https://15m.com/affiliate-programs/betmgm-affiliate/)
- **Income Access** is a real, established third-party affiliate network/platform used by multiple sportsbook operators — one application can potentially cover more than one operator relationship rather than applying to each separately. Worth investigating directly as a single entry point.
- **Licensing is a real, material requirement in 8 states regardless of which operator you work with: Arizona, Colorado, Indiana, Louisiana (revenue-dependent), Michigan, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia.** [Sports Betting Affiliate Marketing 2026 — BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/)
- **The commission model matters a lot for compliance burden.** CPA (a fixed fee per referred depositing player) is comparatively simple to register for. Revenue-share affiliates can face licensing requirements approaching what the operator itself carries, and that licensing process alone can run into the tens of thousands of dollars. **Recommendation: pursue CPA deals only, at least for a v1.**
- Even in states with no licensing requirement, **responsible-gambling disclosures and age-restriction/minor-targeting rules still apply** to any affiliate content.

**This is not legal advice, and I'm not qualified to give it.** Before signing anything in Path B, get a real read from someone who does gaming-law compliance on: which states our actual audience sits in, whether that triggers licensing, and what disclosure language the site would need to carry. This is exactly the kind of decision where "get it wrong" has real regulatory consequences, not just a bad business outcome.

**Also worth weighing:** a prominent sportsbook affiliate placement could complicate the Android/iOS app-store distribution path discussed separately (D-066) — both major app stores have real, specific gambling-content review policies. Not a reason to avoid Path B, but a reason to sequence it deliberately rather than in parallel with an app-store submission.
