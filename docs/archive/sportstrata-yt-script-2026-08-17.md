## Body (talking points, ~150–250 words spoken)

1. **The White Sox lead the AL Central at 65-58 (.528).** That's not a great record by normal standards — it's just better than everyone else in a genuinely weak division. Detroit, Minnesota, and Cleveland are all bunched at 60-64/60-65, five to six games back. Two years removed from a 121-loss season, Chicago is currently a first-place team.

2. **The actual best record in baseball belongs to Milwaukee — 77-48, a .616 winning percentage, run differential of +136.** That's better than the Dodgers (74-51) and better than Atlanta (74-50), the two teams usually eating up the national coverage. The Brewers are on pace for roughly 100 wins and it's barely a story.

3. **There's a legitimate three-way home run race with six weeks left:** Kyle Schwarber (Phillies) leads with 37, Matt Olson (Braves) has 36, Yordan Alvarez (Astros) has 36. Any of the three could still finish on top.

4. **Alvarez isn't just in the power race — he's doing it while hitting .321, tops in MLB**, and he leads all of baseball in rOBA at .443. Power and average at the same time, in the same season, is rare. That's a real MVP argument, not a hype-post one.

5. **The Cubs are 5 games back of Milwaukee in the NL Central (72-53) but sit +5.0 games up in the Wild Card race.** Losing a division and being comfortably in the playoff picture anyway is exactly the kind of thing a standings table shows clearly and a highlight reel doesn't.
