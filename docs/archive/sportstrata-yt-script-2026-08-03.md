## Body (~200 words spoken)

Today was the 2026 trade deadline, and the number that should stop you is Tarik Skubal's line with Detroit before the trade: 7 and 5, a 2.79 ERA, 116 strikeouts against just 14 walks, and a 0.91 WHIP across 96 and two-thirds innings. A WHIP under 1.00 means opposing hitters are barely reaching base — that's ace-of-aces territory, and it's exactly why the Dodgers gave up outfielder Zyhir Hope and pitchers River Ryan and Brady Smith to get him, even while already running away with the NL West.

Now flip to the other end of the deadline: Freddy Peralta. Last year he finished fifth in NL Cy Young voting. This year, after the Brewers sent him to the Mets, his ERA with New York ballooned to 4.99 over 113 and two-thirds innings — and the Mets just flipped him again, to Tampa Bay, as a pure two-month rental. Two trades, one season, for a guy who was a top-five Cy Young arm twelve months ago. That's how fast a pitching stock can crater.

Same day, Kevin Gausman landed with the Cubs and Luis García Jr. joined the Yankees — both stretch-run reinforcements as contenders retool their rosters before the finish line.
