[UCLA](/ncaaf/team/26/ucla-bruins) beat [Illinois](/ncaaf/team/356/illinois-fighting-illini) 22-14 on Friday to reach 10-0, the 3rd 10-0 start in program history and the first since 1998, per UCLA. The other 10-0 starts were 1946 and 1998.

The defense did it. UCLA held Illinois scoreless in the first half, to 240 total yards, and picked off [Katin Houser](/ncaaf/player/4795295/katin-houser) 3 times — 2 by [Scooter Jackson](/ncaaf/player/5152410/scooter-jackson). Illinois averaged 28.4 points a game over its first 9 games; it scored 14, both touchdowns coming in the 2nd half. UCLA led 10-0 at halftime, and a safety in the 3rd quarter made it 12-0. [Aidan Mizell](/ncaaf/player/4917945/aidan-mizell) caught 6 passes for 176 yards, including a 54-yard touchdown in the 1st quarter, and [Ty Dieffenbach](/ncaaf/player/5092275/ty-dieffenbach) threw for 232 yards and 2 touchdowns.

UCLA is one of 2 unbeaten teams left in the FBS, alongside No. 2 Utah at 9-0, and the only team with 10 wins. The Bruins went 3-9 last season; they have 7 more wins than a year ago. Their closest call was a 17-15 win over No. 13 Oregon in Week 6.

UCLA allows 14.1 points a game, the 8th-fewest in the FBS, and has outscored opponents 324-141. Illinois arrived on a 4-game winning streak. UCLA is 7-0 in conference play and closes with a trip to Michigan and a home game against No. 15 USC.
