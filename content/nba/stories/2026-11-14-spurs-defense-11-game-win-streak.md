After an 0-2 start, the [San Antonio Spurs](/nba/team/sas) have won 11 straight, and the streak is built on defense. San Antonio has allowed 104.6 points a game during the 11 wins, 10.4 fewer than the 115.0 those same opponents average in their other games, per ESPN's standings and scoreboard data. The Spurs are 11-2.

The latest win was the starkest. The [Golden State Warriors](/nba/team/gs) scored 85 points in Friday's 123-85 loss, 34.3 below the 119.3 the Warriors average in their other games and their lowest total of the season by 20 points — their next-lowest is 105. The 38-point margin was San Antonio's largest of the streak, ahead of a 27-point win over Sacramento on October 27.

It is not one matchup. 7 of the 8 teams San Antonio has faced in the streak averaged fewer points against the Spurs than in their other games; only Chicago, with 119 points, beat its 113.9 norm.

The suppression travels: 5 of the 11 wins came at home and 6 on the road, and the streak's average margin is 17.1 points a game. [Stephon Castle](/nba/player/4845367/stephon-castle) scored 32 points in 25 minutes on Friday, making 12 of 18 shots, including 5 of 8 from deep, and [Victor Wembanyama](/nba/player/5104157/victor-wembanyama) added 12 points, 12 rebounds, 6 assists and 2 blocks in 21 minutes.

San Antonio's defense is not a one-game story: the Spurs allow 106.2 points a game on the season, the 4th-fewest in the league behind Boston's 103.1, New York's 103.7 and Minnesota's 105.9. Their plus-13.3 differential trails only Minnesota's plus-13.4.

The gap will narrow — 10.4 points is a wide margin to hold all season, and Golden State's 85 was an outlier even inside this streak. But the test already answers the schedule objection: these opponents score less against San Antonio than they score against everyone else.
