The [Minnesota Timberwolves](/nba/standings) have a plus-14.8 point differential per game, the best of the NBA's 30 teams, per ESPN's standings. Next is the [Memphis Grizzlies](/nba/standings) at plus-12.0. Minnesota is 4-2, and its total margin of 89 points is also the league's largest, ahead of Houston at 66 and Memphis at 60.

## Game by game

- at Heat: won 131-106
- at Magic: won 115-93
- Raptors: won 117-94
- at Cavaliers: lost 125-127
- Warriors: lost 114-119
- Clippers: won 127-101

The shape of the record is the story. Minnesota's 4 wins came by 25, 22, 23 and 26 points, while its 2 losses came by 2 and 5 points. The win over the Clippers on October 30 was the latest blowout: a 26-point margin. Minnesota has scored at least 114 points in all 6 games and allowed 119 or fewer in 5 of them.

## Not just a soft schedule

The skeptic's case is the opposition: 3 of Minnesota's 6 opponents have losing records. But those same opponents score more against everyone else. Across their 26 other games, Minnesota's 6 opponents average 116.2 points, compared with 106.7 against Minnesota. The offense helps too: Minnesota scores 121.5 points per game, behind only Portland at 122.8, so the differential is built on both ends.

## The caveat

The caveat: 6 games is 6 games, and a differential lead is not a record lead. Memphis is 5-0 and has lost no games, while Minnesota has lost 2. What can be said: through 6 games, no team in the NBA outscores its opponents by more per game than Minnesota, and the wins have been blowouts while the losses have been close.
