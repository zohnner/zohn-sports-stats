The [Milwaukee Brewers](/mlb/team/mil) swept the [Los Angeles Dodgers](/mlb/team/lad) 4-0 in the NLCS, per SportStrata's schedule data, outscoring Los Angeles 21-11 to reach the World Series.

Game by game: a 2-1 pitchers' duel in the opener (6 hits to 7), a 7-4 win built on 12 hits in Game 2, then 6-2 and 6-4 wins at Dodger Stadium to finish it. Milwaukee won 2 different ways — 2 runs were enough in Game 1, and 12 runs across the final 2 games slammed the door.

The mechanism was run prevention. The Dodgers scored 11 runs in 4 games, 2.75 per game, against a regular-season rate of 4.94. Los Angeles never scored more than 4 in a game. Milwaukee's staff held a 100-win offense under 3 runs per game for a week.

The skeptic's case: this wasn't an upset. Milwaukee won 103 games, the best record in baseball, 3 more than the Dodgers. True — but the Brewers took all 4 anyway, 2 of them on the road, and the Dodgers' bats never got going.

The caveat: 4 games is 4 games. The Dodgers' 4.94 runs per game didn't disappear — Milwaukee suppressed it for a week. The World Series — against a Tampa Bay staff that just held Cleveland to 11 runs in 5 games — is a new test. But a sweep is a sweep: 4-0, 21-11.
