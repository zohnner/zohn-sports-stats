After scoring 20 runs in 3 games to win the ALDS, the [Cleveland Guardians](/mlb/team/cle) scored 11 runs across the final 5 games of the ALCS, per SportStrata's schedule data. The [Tampa Bay Rays](/mlb/team/tb) won the series 4-2 and are going to the World Series.

Game by game: Cleveland opened with a 7-3 win on 14 hits, then Tampa's staff took over. Game 2 went to the Rays 3-1. Cleveland took Game 3 by 3-2 at home. Then 3 straight Tampa wins: 7-3, 3-1, and 7-3 in the clincher. (Game 4 was rained out, pushing the series back a day.)

The mechanism is hits without sequencing. Over the final 5 games Cleveland collected 32 hits and turned them into 11 runs, never scoring more than 3 in a game. In Game 5 the Rays outhit Cleveland 12-5 and needed only 3 runs. In the regular season the Guardians averaged 4.19 runs per game; Tampa held them to 2.2 for 5 straight games.

The skeptic's case is that Cleveland just went cold — 14 hits in Game 1 shows the lineup was fine. Maybe. But the Guardians had just scored 20 runs on 27 hits in 3 games against the White Sox, and Tampa cut that production nearly in half for a full week. Cold had help.

The caveat: 6 games is 6 games, and a week of great pitching doesn't guarantee the next round. The World Series — against a Milwaukee team that just swept the Dodgers — is a new test. But the ALCS turned on one fact: after Game 1, the hottest lineup in October scored 11 runs in 5 games.
