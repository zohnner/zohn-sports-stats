The [Kansas City Chiefs](/nfl/team/kc) placed Josh Simmons on injured reserve on Thursday, and vice president of sports medicine Rick Burkholder opened the press conference with the file the public had never been given. In mid-August, Simmons reported calf discomfort and could not post on the leg that had knee surgery at Ohio State. An MRI found a bulging, herniated disc in his lumbar spine, higher up than the team usually sees in its linemen. A California spine specialist recommended rehab over surgery, a later MRI showed the disc had improved, and Simmons returned to limited practice last week — then plateaued short of a return to competition. Surgery is still not recommended. He will miss at least 4 more games, with the earliest return in Week 11.

## What fans heard instead

- August: team sources described the back problem as a non-issue, and Simmons sat out the preseason hoping to be ready for the opener.
- September: week-to-week, with information scarce enough that ESPN reporter Jeremy Fowler called the injury mysterious.
- Late September: Andy Reid named the bulging disc and said it was sapping strength from Simmons' legs, while calling his progress a move in the right direction.
- October: a limited practice return, then Reid describing Simmons as still ramping up on Monday of this week.
- Wednesday: Simmons was listed as both back and personal, after Reid omitted him from the morning rundown of non-participants — an omission a team spokesperson later called inadvertent. Burkholder said Thursday that the personal day was spent talking the injured reserve decision over with family and representatives.

## Why the desire question stuck

Simmons has played 8 games in his career. In the 2025 season, he missed 4 games over a 22-day absence the team called a family situation, and he kept the details in-house when he returned. That history is the lens fans were handed, and it is exactly why vagueness was never going to be neutral in his case. Player privacy can protect a diagnosis. It cannot explain why a team protects a timeline it controls, or why it lets effort become the open question. The Chiefs could have said in September what Burkholder said on Thursday: Simmons was in the building daily, working with the training staff, and the open question was leg strength, not desire. Said at a podium, that sentence costs nothing private. Left to insider whispers, it never reached the fans doing the judging.

The front office set that strategy, and Reid's podium updates delivered it — each one sounding nearer to a return than the medical file, as laid out on Thursday, ever supported. Burkholder's repeated praise on Thursday for how diligently Simmons worked every day was the right defense of the player. It arrived after the personal designation had already let the worst reading of his absence run for a full news cycle.

## The caveat

Disc rehab is not linear, and the medical arc in Thursday's account is coherent: an improved MRI argued against surgery, the plateau only showed under practice load, and injured reserve is the conservative outcome. What does not hold up is the public arc — a serious August diagnosis presented as minor, then a fall spent at week-to-week. A legitimate injury was allowed to look like a motivation problem. That was a choice, and it was made in Kansas City's front office, not in Simmons' rehab sessions.
