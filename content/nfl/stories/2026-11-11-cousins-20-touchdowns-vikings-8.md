Kirk Cousins has thrown 20 touchdown passes in 9 games, the 2nd-most in the NFL behind Joe Burrow's 23. The [Minnesota Vikings](/nfl/team/min) have thrown 8 in 8 games, and have scored 12 touchdowns in total. Cousins alone has thrown more touchdown passes than Minnesota's entire offense has scored.

The yardage gap is nearly as wide. Cousins has thrown for 2,340 yards, 5th in the NFL and 260.0 a game, completing 202 of 315 passes for 64.1%. Minnesota's 3 quarterbacks have combined for 1,437 yards, 179.6 a game. Cousins averages 2.2 touchdown passes a game; Minnesota's quarterbacks average 1.0. By expected points added, Cousins is at 54.4; Minnesota's quarterbacks are at -30.8.

## 3 quarterbacks, 8 touchdowns

Minnesota has used 3 quarterbacks. Kyler Murray has thrown for 1,022 yards and 5 touchdowns, Carson Wentz for 357 and 3, and Max Brosmer for 58 and none. Together they have thrown 5 interceptions.

The other side of the ledger: Cousins has thrown 8 interceptions, more than the 5 Minnesota's 3 quarterbacks have thrown combined. He throws touchdowns at a rate Minnesota has not had, and interceptions at a rate it has not had either. The 8 interceptions sit beside the 20 touchdowns; both numbers are the point.

## A 6-2 team with a 12-touchdown offense

Minnesota is 6-2 while scoring 146 points in 8 games, 18.3 a game, on the strength of a defense that has allowed 116. The [Las Vegas Raiders](/nfl/team/lv) are 4-5. The numbers say what Minnesota is getting in the passing game: a quarterback who has thrown 20 touchdown passes in 9 games for a 4-5 team, joining a 6-2 team whose quarterbacks have thrown 8 in 8. Whether that closes the gap between Minnesota's record and its offense is a question for the games, not this page.
