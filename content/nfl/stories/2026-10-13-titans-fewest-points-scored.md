Through Week 5, the [Tennessee Titans](/nfl/team/ten) have scored 58 points, the fewest of the league's 32 teams, per SportStrata's standings data. That's 11.6 per game in a season where the average team scores 23.3 — Tennessee is producing at almost exactly half the league rate.

Game by game: lost 23-10 to the Jets in the opener, 24-20 to the Eagles, 12-7 to the Giants, 24-18 to the Ravens, and 26-3 to the Texans on Sunday. 3 of the 5 losses came by 6 or fewer points (4, 5, and 6), and Tennessee has been held to 10 or fewer points in 3 of the 5 games.

Here's the disconnect: the defense isn't the problem. The Titans have allowed 109 points, tied for the 10th-fewest in the league — a top-10 unit by the only measure that matters. They're 0-5 because one side of the ball is top-10 and the other sits dead last. Miami, the next-lowest scoring team, has 70 points — 12 more than Tennessee.

The skeptic's case writes itself: 0-5 is 0-5, and teams this unproductive on offense rarely turn it around mid-season. Fair. But the margins say this isn't a roster-wide collapse — it's one unit. Tennessee doesn't need a good offense to win games. It needs a below-average one instead of the league's least productive, because the defense is already keeping the score close.

The caveat: 5 games is 5 games, and moral victories don't count in the standings. Still, no team in football is getting less from its offense right now — and Tennessee pairs the league's worst scoring with a top-10 defense.
