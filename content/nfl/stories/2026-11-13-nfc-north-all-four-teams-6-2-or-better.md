All 4 teams in the NFC North are 6-2 or better through Week 9, per ESPN's standings — the first time any division has done that in any season since 1999, per nflverse. The [Chicago Bears](/nfl/team/chi) lead at 7-2, the [Minnesota Vikings](/nfl/team/min) and [Detroit Lions](/nfl/team/det) are 6-2, and the [Green Bay Packers](/nfl/team/gb) are 6-3. Together they are 25-9.

The closest any division came in those 27 seasons was a weakest team with 5 wins, which happened 6 times: the 2008 NFC East, the 2014 AFC North, the 2021 AFC North, the 2021 AFC West, the 2022 AFC East and the 2023 AFC North.

Chicago leads the division without winning in it. The Bears lost 9-3 to Minnesota in Week 2 and 31-7 at Green Bay in Week 5, and they are 1 of 3 teams since 1999 to reach 7-2 or better by the end of Week 9 without a division win. The others are Denver last season, 7-2 with a 0-1 division record, and Buffalo this season, 7-1 with a 0-1 division record. Detroit is 2-0 in the division, Minnesota 2-1 and Green Bay 1-2.

The division will be decided inside it: Chicago and Detroit have 4 division games left each, Minnesota and Green Bay have 3 each, and 7 division games remain in total.

Detroit and Minnesota have played 8 games each to Chicago's and Green Bay's 9, so the records are through Week 9 rather than through an equal number of games. And the history reaches back to 1999, as far as the game file goes — not to the start of the league.
